# Force Multiplier 22.5.0 — 20-Level Visual Game

## Product structure

Five functions now contain 20 sequential levels each. The original 100-skill curriculum remains the underlying map, but the learner sees one level at a time. One successful completion unlocks the next level; completed levels remain replayable with new seeded variations up to 10 tracked successes.

Inside a level the app remains in Focus Mode: global navigation, function tabs, skill lists and other scenarios are hidden. The active work product, its controls, feedback, outcome animation and Exit Scenario control remain visible.

Levels 1–5 use the same lineup/turn interaction while new conditions accumulate. Levels 6–20 use a progressively more constrained weekly checkerboard. A successful Scheduling level can launch an execution animation: lineups show the first launch and turn launch; checkerboard levels animate the week from Monday through Friday.

## Analysis progression

Twenty levels progress from framing the question and choosing the population through metrics, reporting, comparison, distributions, trends, baselines, signals, drivers, display choices, hypotheses, analytical tests, uncertainty, operational importance, forecasting, scenario comparison, briefing and outcome verification.

## Documentation progression

Twenty levels progress from finding the controlling requirement through applicability, due points, recurrence, prerequisites, routing, evidence, workflow state, closeout, partial work, unresolved items, exceptions, requirement changes, version control, corrections, transfers, handover and retention.

## Data Integrity progression

Twenty levels progress from source authority and record identity through scope/timing, normalization, field rules, units/dates, mappings, missing values, duplicates, relationships, broken references, two-way membership reconciliation, attribute comparison, event logic, independent totals, provenance, controlled correction, downstream verification, unresolved exceptions and prevention.

## Database & Systems progression

Twenty levels progress from workflow definition and information structure through configuration, permissions, provisioning, lifecycle, access review, positive/negative testing, failure-layer diagnosis, misleading views, incident reconstruction, imports, interfaces, configuration change, restoration, outage continuity, backlog reconciliation, monitoring, escalation and verified closure.

## Training claim boundary

Level completion is game progress, not official occupational qualification. Repetition and the increasingly integrated work products are designed to develop fluency, but real-world transfer and proficiency require human validation with actual learners and practitioners.
